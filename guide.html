How it works

Draw a doodle or upload a picture, and the grid on the right turns it into a pieced quilt pattern: a fabric list, cutting sizes, yardage, and a row-by-row piecing guide.

  1. Pick Draw or Upload in the input window.
  2. Set how many blocks across/down you want, how many fabrics (colors) to use, and your finished block size.
  3. The pattern updates as you go — or hit Make my quilt to force a refresh.
  4. Switch to Pattern chart for a numbered, symbol-keyed chart you can print or follow while piecing.

Drawing tools

Block
Fills one whole grid square per click — the most reliable way to get crisp, gap-free results. Drag to fill a run of squares; right-click to erase a square.
Pencil / Eraser
Freehand drawing. Keep your lines at least as thick as one grid square, or they can get lost during conversion.
Line / Rectangle / Ellipse
Hold Shift while dragging to snap a line to 45°, or constrain a rectangle/ellipse to a square/circle.
Paint bucket
Flood-fills a connected area with the current color.
Eyedropper
Picks up a color already on the canvas.
tips.txt

Tips for a clean pattern

Use the Block tool. It fills exact grid squares with no anti-aliasing, so what you draw is exactly what converts — no surprise gaps or blurred edges.
Keep it bold and high-contrast. Simple shapes with a handful of solid colors convert far more cleanly than soft gradients or busy detail.
Watch your fabric count. Fewer Max fabrics means fewer colors to cut and piece — start around 4–8 and raise it only if your design needs it.
Tune the line threshold. When drawing, a lower threshold keeps thin outlines from disappearing; a higher one smooths out stray pixels for cleaner edges.
Start with a smaller grid. A 16×16 or 24×24 block grid is much faster to piece than a 45×45 one — size up only once you like the shape.
Photos work best simplified. For uploads, images with bold shapes and few colors (logos, silhouettes, clip art) convert better than detailed photos. Try Crisp (graphics) sampling for flat art and Smooth (photos) for photographs.
ideas.txt

Project ideas

Not sure what to make? Here are a few starting points, roughly easiest to most ambitious:

Heart or starClassic, symmetric, and forgiving — a great first quilt to learn the tools on.
Monogram / initialsDraw a bold letter or two with the Block tool for a personalized baby quilt or gift.
Bunny or cat silhouetteA simple sitting-animal shape with ears and a rounded body reads clearly even at a small grid size.
Geometric blockDiamonds, chevrons, or a pinwheel — use Line/Rectangle with Shift-snap for crisp angles.
Seasonal motifSnowflake, pumpkin, ornament, or a simple tree — upload a clip-art reference and trace over it with the Block tool.
Pet or logo uploadUpload a high-contrast photo or logo and let the converter do the work; simplify colors down until the shape reads clearly.
SamplerDivide your grid into quadrants and draw a different small motif in each for a scrappy sampler quilt.
